# Additional articles: Heat vulnerability → GI prioritization / spatial decision support

## 12. Brenner, Schmidt & Albert (2023) — Heat vulnerability → roof-greening prioritization

**Brenner, J., Schmidt, S., & Albert, C. (2023).** “Localizing and prioritizing roof greening opportunities for urban heat island mitigation: insights from the city of Krefeld, Germany.” *Landscape Ecology, 38*, 1697–1712.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10980-023-01644-8

**Why it is important:** The study uses a three-step design: (1) examine residents’ vulnerability to heat, (2) assess existing green roofs and potentials for greening, and (3) prioritize opportunity spaces for roof greening. This is one of the closest precedents for translating heat-vulnerability information into a subsequent GI-prioritization stage.

**Relevance:** Very high.

## 13. Sanchez & Reames (2019) — LST + socioeconomic vulnerability → green-roof need

**Sanchez, L., & Reames, T. G. (2019).** “Cooling Detroit: A socio-spatial analysis of equity in green roofs as an urban heat island mitigation strategy.” *Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 44*, 126331.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ufug.2019.04.014

**Why it is important:** The study uses land surface temperature, income and race to identify areas where green-roof cooling benefits are most needed and examines the spatial relationship between vulnerability and green-roof planning.

**Relevance:** Very high.

## 14. Reinstaller, König & Muschalla (2025) — hazard maps → BGI priority map

**Reinstaller, S., König, A. W., & Muschalla, D. (2025).** “Using High-Resolution Flood Hazard and Urban Heat Island Maps for High-Priority BGI Placement at the City Scale.” *Hydrology, 12*(5), 125.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/hydrology12050125

**Why it is important:** The paper presents a workflow for creating a blue-green infrastructure priority map by combining urban heat-island and flood-hazard information. It is a direct precedent for translating spatial hazard information into a transparent BGI priority map.

**Relevance:** Very high for the prioritization stage.

## 15. Chang et al. (2021) — spatial vulnerability decision support

**Chang, H., Pallathadka, A., Sauer, J., Grimm, N. B., Zimmerman, R., Cheng, C., Iwaniec, D. M., Kim, Y., Lloyd, R., McPhearson, T., Rosenzweig, B., Troxler, T., Welty, C., Brenner, R., & Herreros-Cantis, P. (2021).** “Assessment of urban flood vulnerability using the social-ecological-technological systems framework in six US cities.” *Sustainable Cities and Society, 68*, 102786.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2021.102786

**Note:** This is a supporting methodological precedent for spatial vulnerability and decision support, but it focuses on flood vulnerability rather than heat/GI siting. Use it as supporting methodology rather than as a direct GI precedent.

**Relevance:** Moderate.

## 16. Sobhaninia et al. (2025) — systematic review of GI siting methods

**Sobhaninia, S., Meerow, S., Dugger, A., Hopson, T., He, C., & Wilhelmi, O. (2025).** “Where should the green go? A systematic literature review of methods for siting green infrastructure to mitigate rising heat and stormwater risks in cities worldwide.” *Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 107*, 128790.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ufug.2025.128790

**Why it is important:** This is the key review for the GI-prioritization methodology. It reviewed 78 relevant studies and found that heat-oriented GI siting commonly uses remotely sensed LST, demographic variables, LULC and other spatial criteria. It also identifies heat-vulnerability indices and multi-criteria decision analysis among approaches used for GI spatial planning.

**Relevance:** Essential methodological reference.

## 17. Methodological implication for the MSc thesis

The literature supports a structure in which heat-related spatial evidence is assessed first and then translated into GI planning priorities. A defensible thesis workflow is therefore:

> **GeoAI → urban heat vulnerability → transparent GI prioritization → GI priority map**

rather than:

> **LST + NDVI + NDBI + socioeconomic variables → artificially labelled GI priority → ML prediction**

The second approach would require an independently defensible training target for “GI priority.” If the labels are created from the same criteria used as predictors, the model would largely learn the researcher’s own rules rather than independently predict observed GI priority.

### Important limitation

These papers do **not** establish one universal formula for a GI priority score. The specific criteria, thresholds and/or weights for Greater Accra still need to be justified from the GI literature and the planning objectives of this study.
