## 2. 🌍 Climate Protection Need and Potential

### 2.1 🏭 Greenhouse-Gas Emissions

The City's **2030 Climate Neutrality Action Plan** reports the following **2019 baseline**:

**Total: 3,669,191 tonnes CO₂-equivalent per year (t CO₂e/year).**

| Sector | Emissions (t CO₂e/year) | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Buildings and heating | 1,463,854 | 40% |
| Electricity | 1,018,618 | 28% |
| Transport | 839,688 | 23% |
| Other (including IPPU and AFOLU) | 293,639 | 8% |
| Waste | 53,392 | 1% |
| **Total** | **3,669,191** | **100%** |

**Date caveat:** The Action Plan says the latest comprehensive inventory available for its preparation was based on **2020 data**, while **2019** was selected as the Climate City Contract baseline. The table is the 2019 baseline, not a 2025/2026 measurement.

Dortmund's Climate City Contract sets a target of at least **80% greenhouse-gas emissions reduction by 2030 compared with 2019**.

### 2.2 🌳 CO₂ Fixation Capacity

The Action Plan identifies forests, parks, gardens, trees, green roofs and façades as green infrastructure that can contribute to carbon binding and climate adaptation.

Figures stated in the Action Plan:
- Around **50% of Dortmund's area** consists of parks, forests, agricultural land and open spaces.
- The Parks and Green Spaces Department manages approximately **9.35 million m²** of green areas, including around **150,000 urban trees**.
- Dortmund's forests cover up to **14% of the city area**; the plan reports an increase of **77% since the 1960s**.

**Limitation:** The Action Plan says exact CO₂ fixation is difficult to quantify and depends on location, plant species, growth phase and maintenance. A verified citywide annual CO₂ fixation figure has not yet been identified.

**Source:** [Dortmund 2030 Climate Neutrality Action Plan (PDF, 2025)](https://www.dortmund.de/dortmund/projekte/rathaus/verwaltung/amt-fuer-angelegenheiten-des-oberbuergermeisters-und-des-rates/bilder/ciio-chief-information-innovation-office/mission-klima/2025-10-30_action-plan_dortmund.pdf)

## 3. ☀️ Exposition

### 3.1 ☀️ Sun Exposition — Duration and Spatially Exposed Areas

#### 🌞 Sunshine Duration

A DWD-data-based published summary for Dortmund reports:
- **2025 annual sunshine duration: 1,788 hours.**
- **1991–2020 long-term annual average: 1,550 hours.**
- 2025 was approximately **238 hours above** the 1991–2020 average.

**Caveats:** The 2025 figure is a gridded DWD estimate based on a 3 × 3 group of 1-km grid cells around the municipality centre. It is not a measurement from one local station or one of the selected project-area sensors. Sunshine duration is not the same as global solar radiation.

The source's monthly figures refer to **1991–2020 monthly normals**, not 2025 monthly observations.

**Source lead:** [Sonnenstunden Dortmund — DWD gridded-data summary](https://www.sonnenstunden.de/) — verify the exact page and underlying DWD dataset before final academic citation.

#### 📍 Areas Identified as Strongly Sun-Exposed in Innenstadt-Ost

The **Regionalverband Ruhr / City of Dortmund Climate Analysis (October 2019)** identifies specific surface types and locations where strong solar exposure and lack of shade are relevant.

| Exposed area / surface | What the report identifies | Planning implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Strongly sun-exposed inner courtyards** in densely built residential and mixed-use areas | The report recommends planting shade-providing trees in these courtyards to create climate refuges. | Plant trees and create shaded courtyard spaces. |
| **Large, unshaded car parks** in commercial areas **Im Spähenfelde-Nord and Körne** | The report describes these car parks as unshaded and recommends large-canopy trees to reduce solar exposure. | Prioritise tree shade and vegetation on parking surfaces. |
| **Commercial and industrial areas along the northern boundary**, including **Im Spähenfelde-West/-Ost, Körne, Großmarkt, Brinkstraße and Brunnenstraße** | High surface sealing and lack of shade trees are associated with strong overheating. | Increase vegetation; shade yards and parking areas; consider roof and façade greening. |
| **Schoolyards, parking areas and public open spaces** | The report names **Landgrafen-Grundschule**, the parking area at **Polizeipräsidium**, and the almost completely unshaded lawn east of **Südbad** as places where shade trees should be planted. | Target these spaces for shade provision. |
| **Street corridors** | The report recommends retaining or planting shade trees along **Saarlandstraße, Heiliger Weg and the northern part of Hohe Straße**. | Improve shade along exposed street spaces. |
| **Street edges / street margins** | The report specifically names the **northern section of Hellweg** for street greening. | Add street trees and greening where space and road-safety conditions allow. |

**Important distinction:** These are areas and surface types described in the 2019 Dortmund climate-planning report as unshaded, strongly sun-exposed or subject to overheating. The report does **not** provide a separate quantitative solar-exposure map with irradiance values for every individual parcel. Do not claim that every part of a named district or street is equally exposed.

**Primary source:** [Regionalverband Ruhr / City of Dortmund — Klimaanalyse Stadt Dortmund (October 2019)](https://www.dortmund.de/dortmund/projekte/rathaus/verwaltung/umweltamt/downloads/klimaschutz/klimafolgeanpassung/klimaanalyse_stadt_dortmund_oktober-2019.pdf) — consult the Innenstadt-Ost discussion and planning recommendations, particularly the relevant pages around pp. 236–244.

### 3.3 💧 Humidity Hotspots and Temporality

Dortmund's municipal climate sensor network comprises **105 sensors at 76 locations** and provides temperature, relative humidity, wind, radiation, precipitation and other parameters. Measurements are updated every five minutes.

**Sensor interpretation:**
- **SHT35 and ATM41:** measure direct air temperature and are comparable for that variable.
- **Black Globe:** includes radiation influence and can produce different values; do not treat its globe temperature as conventional air temperature.
- Solar-radiation measurement availability depends on the installed sensor type; do not assume every site measures radiation.

The municipal weather station at Brückstraße 45 also measures solar radiation and other weather variables; its online historical data are available through 2023.

**Important limitation:** The existence of the sensor network does not by itself prove which locations are humidity hotspots. A hotspot claim needs evidence for the relevant places and time periods.

**Sources**
- [Open Data Dortmund — Current climate sensor measurements](https://open-data.dortmund.de/explore/dataset/klimasensoren-aktuelle-messungen/)

## 6. ⚠️ Reporting Reminders

- Label every statistic with its **year**, **geographical coverage** and **measurement/reference period**.
- Do not call 1991–2020 climate normals “2025 data”.
- Do not treat a gridded municipal estimate as a measurement at a particular sensor or neighbourhood.
- Do not equate sunshine duration with solar radiation.
- Do not equate Black Globe temperature with standard air temperature.
- Do not claim a humidity hotspot without evidence for that place and time.
- Do not infer a quantified CO₂ sink from the existence or area of green infrastructure.
- Where evidence is missing, leave the item marked **🟡 REMAINING** rather than inventing a value.
